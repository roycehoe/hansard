# Launch fast-track plan: speeding up the Parliament permission request

Prepared 2026-10-18. Research only. Nothing here has been sent, and nobody has been contacted. This builds on `plans/launch-q3-permission.md` (terms, law, Clerk channel, precedents, draft letter), which is not repeated here.

Reading rules: claims backed only by a search snippet, or inferred by me, are marked [unverified]. Event dates come from official pages where I could fetch them. WebFetch returns summaries, so re-read each page before acting on a date or a price.

## The short version

1. No senior person can grant this except the Clerk of Parliament and, above him, the Speaker. Every other route can only make the Clerk's office notice the letter, or tell Royce how to write it so it gets a yes. That is the useful ceiling.
2. The informal route should not run ahead of the letter. Send the letter first, get a ticket or acknowledgement, then use that reference in every conversation. A senior person who is asked "can you help with this?" needs something to point at.
3. The best contacts, in order: the Pair team (they already did what Royce is asking, with Parliament), then Alwyn Tan as a source of introductions and advice only, then Royce's own MP as a low-expectation side channel. The Speaker, Ministers and the press are off the list.
4. The events list is thin. I found few public, dated events in the next six months that put him near these people. The honest plan leans on a small number of direct, official approaches and treats events as a bonus.
5. The biggest risk is not rudeness. It is a conflict with the letter's own text: the draft says he has shared the site only with "a small private test group". Networking with a login in hand can break that sentence.

### Own MP and the Meet-the-People Session

How it works, from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet-the-People_Sessions (secondary source): sessions are one-to-one meetings with elected MPs, weekly, in the evening, at a constituency office. A volunteer writer drafts a letter to the relevant ministry, statutory board or other party, the MP vets and signs it, and standard letters go out in about three working days. The same article says the Civil Service gives these letters higher priority because they come from elected MPs, including opposition MPs. Residents of a GRC may see any MP of that GRC. There is no official procedure document I could find for how agencies must treat these letters.

Does it fit this ask? Poorly, and I would not lead with it.

- The sessions are built for personal cases: housing, CPF, fees, licences, financial hardship. A request to republish a public record is a project, not a constituent hardship.
- The addressee is the Parliament Secretariat, which belongs to the legislature. The priority agencies give MP letters is a civil service habit. Whether it carries over to a Secretariat letter is unknown [unverified].
- An MP who is asked to write for a personal website may decline, or may write a short note that reads as pressure. Either leaves a mark on the file.
- Where it could help: an MP has standing to raise a matter with the Clerk in a way a member of the public does not. If Royce's own MP takes an interest, a one-line "I know this person, please read the request carefully" is worth more than any event contact.

If Royce wants to try it, the ask is advice, not a letter: "I have written to the Clerk under clause 6. Is there anything you would advise before I follow up?" Use the MP's published office email, not a queue. Keep the MPS visit for after the letter is out, and only if the MP does not reply by email. Do not hand the MP a draft letter to send. Royce does not need an MP to "forward" the request, and it should not be framed as a case.

## Sequenced plan

The rule: the letter goes first and the informal contacts are about the letter, never instead of it.

### Before day 0 (target: by 2 November, before STACK)

1. Fix the scraper to match the letter (User-Agent, pacing, `Retry-After`, incremental only). The base plan lists this. A reply that asks "what do you do at the moment?" has to meet an honest answer.
2. Re-read the terms in a browser, confirm the Clerk, decide what to say about Par-leh-mate. Same list as the base plan.
3. Decide the sharing line. The draft letter says the site is behind a password and shared only with "a small private test group". From now until a reply, keep that true. Do not hand out logins at events. If a contact wants to see the site, they can look after the letter is in, and Royce says so in the letter ("I am happy to give you a login").

### Day 0: formal letter

Send as in the base plan: PDF to parl@parl.gov.sg addressed to the Clerk, plus the feedback form to create a ticket. Keep the reply email and any ticket number. Everything below refers to that record.

### Days 1 to 14: factual question to the Pair team

- Channel: the public Pair support link, from Royce's own name, not through Alwyn.
- Ask: "I built a Hansard reading site and have written to the Clerk under clause 6 of the Parliament terms. How did your team approach Parliament about Pair Search, and is there a better route for a third-party reader? I am not asking you to speak to Parliament for me."
- Do not ask: for a name inside Parliament; for them to pass the letter on; for any statement of support; for API or data access.
- Why: it is a factual question a team can answer without risk. A short reply may tell him what Parliament expects (a licence, a link-out, a referral), which he can fold into a follow-up.

### Days 1 to 21: Alwyn Tan, one conversation, as a friend

- Ask for advice and one pointer: "Who in OGP knows how the Pair and Parliament arrangement was set up, and would they be open to a short question from me? May I say you suggested it?"
- Do not ask him to lobby, to forward the letter, to mention it to Pair, or to speak to Parliament contacts. He does not work on Pair Search, and a public officer asked to vouch for an outsider's request to another institution has nothing to gain and something to lose. Take the pointer, thank him, and make the approach yourself.
- Tell him the letter is already sent. That one sentence tells him he is hearing about a proper process and not asked to substitute for one.

### STACK Conference, 4 to 5 November (optional)

- Aim: one or two short conversations with Pair or OGP staff, if they are there. Use the same question as the support-link message. Ask once, listen, do not pitch the site.
- Do not: collect names to chase later, bring up the letter with any Parliament staff, or leave a link to the site.

### Day 21: formal follow-up (from the base plan)

One polite line, original attached. If the Pair team replied with something useful (for example "the Official Reports team is the right desk"), add one sentence: "I understand the Official Reports team may be the right desk. Please pass the request there if so." This is the only place the informal work shows up in the formal thread, and it asks the Secretariat to route, not decide.

### Day 35: phone call (from the base plan)

Call 6332 6666 and ask who owns the request. If Royce's MP replied helpfully by email, mention only that he has spoken to his MP for advice. Do not name the MP as a sponsor.

### Day 35 to 56: MP step, only if still silent

Email the MP's office as described above, asking for advice, with the letter's date and ticket reference. Keep it to six lines. Attend a Meet-the-People Session only if the office asks him to.

### Day 56: decide (from the base plan)

Final email, then stay private or follow the fallback ladder in the base plan. By this date the informal work has either produced a referral or it has not. Do not extend the deadline for a warm introduction.

## Risks and how to avoid them

1. **Going over the Secretariat's head.** Triggers: writing to the Speaker or a Minister; using a contact to put the request on a senior person's desk before the Clerk's office has replied; telling the Secretariat "I know someone". Avoid: the letter goes to the generic address first, every informal contact is asked for advice, and any reference to the contact in the formal thread is a sentence asking the Secretariat to route, never a name offered as a lever.
2. **Making an OGP officer lobby for him.** Public officers have rules about outside advocacy and about speaking for another institution. If Alwyn feels cornered, the friendship and the Pair relationship both take damage. Ask for a pointer. Never ask him to carry the request.
3. **The Pair overlap.** Pair Search is a government product, and the base plan notes Royce's site duplicates part of it. Approaching Pair for help may read as asking a competitor to bless his entry. Say what the site adds (MP and sitting pages, speaker attribution back to 1955), and ask only how their arrangement works.
4. **Breaking the letter's own claims.** The letter says the site is private and shared only with a small test group. Handing out logins at events, posting screenshots, or demoing it on a phone to ten people makes that false. Also keep the scraper's actual behaviour matching the letter (User-Agent, pacing) before day 0. The Queensland refusal turned on trust, per the base plan.
5. **Using the hackerdrinks or Heartbeat brand.** Both are real communities with their own reputations. Do not introduce himself as "from hackerdrinks" or cite Heartbeat as backing. The request is from Royce, in his own name.
6. **Name-dropping Par-leh-mate.** Settle what to say about it before talking to anyone. Pair and Parliament staff may know it. If it never asked for permission, a casual remark can reach the wrong ears.
7. **Public pressure.** No LinkedIn post, no thread, no "waiting for Parliament" posts while the request is open. A public post is a campaign, and the Secretariat will read it as one.
8. **Over-reading thin evidence.** Several facts here are snippet-level: the Clerk's start date, the Speaker's term, who sits on the Pair team now, and the 2027 hackathon dates. Re-check each before it goes into anything sent.

## What would change my view

- If Pair replies that the arrangement with Parliament was a data feed, not a licence, the base plan's fallback should shift toward asking Parliament whether a Pair-adjacent route exists, since the likely answer to a letter is "use the official tool".
- If the Secretariat acknowledges the letter and names a desk, drop the MP step and any event outreach. The formal route is now working.
- If a trusted person who knows the Secretariat offers an introduction unprompted, take it, but only after the letter is in and with the introducer free to say no.
