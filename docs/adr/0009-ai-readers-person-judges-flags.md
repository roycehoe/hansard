# 0009. AI readers read every row, a person judges the flags

The methods page reports how often a credited paragraph names the wrong person, and so far that figure comes from AI readers alone. A hand check was meant to put a person behind it. The first try, a 50-row review page, drew its rows through the search API, left eight Parliaments with no rows, used only the first or last 100 reports of each Parliament and skipped written answers. An agent saved its 50 marks within one second, so no person read them. On 1 November 2026 Royce decided what the hand check means. Two independent AI readers, one Sonnet and one Opus, judge every row of a fresh, seeded sample of 1,000 credited rows against a written rulebook, and a script compares each row's text with its SPRS paragraph. Royce reads every row that either reader or the script flags, and his verdict is final. An AI reader can add a flag but never clear one. Thirty planted errors, outside the 1,000 and with their key sealed until both readers finish, measure how many errors the readers catch. If either reader misses one, the reader prompt is fixed and both readers run again before anything is published. Rows that both readers and the script pass are never read by a person. The alternative was for Royce to also read 50 random passed rows. It was rejected, because no errors in 50 rows only bounds the share the readers miss at 5.8%. The published claim says that a person read only the flags: "Two AI readers checked 1,000 paragraphs against the Official Report. Royce read every paragraph they flagged, and x were wrong. They caught y of 30 planted errors." The scraper runs the check and publishes all 1,000 rows in its report, and the methods page here gives the ceilings and the planted-error recall, linked to that report. A change that lets an AI reader clear a flag, or that describes the check as a person reading every row, needs a new ADR.

Note, 2 November 2026. Royce judged 15 flags one at a time. For the other 45, flagged for their words only, an AI agent sorted the rows by kind of difference, Royce ruled on each kind after reading examples, and the agent wrote his ruling to each row with the writer `claude-cluster-ruling`. A second agent checked every row against its kind. The methods page states this in place of the claim quoted above.

Note, 2 November 2026. Royce set how often the hand check runs. It runs once a quarter, and after any write that changes more than 1% of credited rows. A repeat check uses one Sonnet reader, the 30 planted errors and the script that compares each row's text with SPRS. The rulebook teaches the reader three kinds of difference that Royce ruled on in the first check: a question number the archive leaves out, a language note the archive strips, and a speaker label left in the source paragraph. The target is a published wrong-credit ceiling of 1% or less. Royce rejected a size floor for Tier A, the serious error kinds, so every Tier A kind a check finds is still fixed.

Note, 14 November 2026. Royce rewrote the methods page for a reader with no background in statistics. It now gives the error rates alone and says in two sentences that an AI and a script read the paragraphs, that Royce decided every flag, reading 3 himself and the rest by rulings he had already made, and how many were credited wrongly. The ceilings and the planted-error recall came off the page. They stay in the check report, which the page reaches through its link to the scraper's report folder.
