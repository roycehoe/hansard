# 0005. Palette from the debating chamber

The first palette came from the outside of Parliament House, the Speaker's gown and the former chamber in the Old Parliament House. Most people know Parliament from the debating chamber they see in the news and on the sitting broadcasts, so on 17 Oct 2026 Royce replaced it with colours sampled from the current chamber. The primary is the mahogany panelling on the wall behind the Speaker's chair. The page is the cream of the floor carpet, panels are the timber of the table of the House, tags are the seat leather, and dark mode sits on the panelling. The states, the search highlight and flag white keep their first-palette sources: the crest, the lawn, the gold lace, the evening sky and the orchid. The two loudest chamber colours were turned down for primary. The seat leather has the gown red's hue, near the PSP and SDP reds. The orange timber on a cream page would be the cream, serif and terracotta look that `app/STYLE.md` lists as an AI tell.

Amended 7 Nov 2026. The primary is no longer the mahogany. Links, buttons and the focus ring use the gold lace from the Speaker's gown (#896016 in light, #c69b57 in dark), because a mahogany link differed from the body text only by lightness, and the gold also differs by hue. Gold at hue 77 is about 35 degrees from a typical terracotta such as #c4633f, and the band and the body stay brown, so the site still sits outside the cream, serif and terracotta cluster. The mahogany panelling now colours the header band and the tags. The line above that tags are the seat leather is retired: the solid tag is the panelling #6d3e31 with white text in light and cream text in dark, and tags in lists are a pale chip with a visible edge. A pale green chip marks a status such as Sitting MP, from the lawn. The seat leather has no token left, and its derived steps are deleted.

No openly licensed photo of the current chamber exists, so this palette breaks the first palette's rule that every source photo has a known open licence and is committed for traceability. The samples come from Parliament's own photo of the empty chamber, rechecked against broadcast frames. The photos are not in the repo. `docs/palette.md` cites each one by URL with its crop box, and the `/palette` page links to the photo instead of showing it. ADR 0002 still applies: colours only, no crest, Mace or other mark sampled or shown. Anyone adding a chamber photo to the repo or the site needs a licence for it first.
