# 0011. Links between records come from fixed rules

Researchers cite this archive, so a link has to be something anyone can reproduce and check. Research for the link work in November 2026 found peer sites using language models to connect records, and the scraper's own Act links already accepted a title pair at 0.85 similarity or more. On 9 November 2026 Royce ruled that every link comes from a fixed rule, so the same data gives the same links on every run. A rule may use a citation or identifier the record prints, an exact comparison after a fixed clean-up (ignoring case and punctuation, or comparing the same words in any order), or a hand-kept list that maps one known misprint to one spelling, such as `AMANDMENT` to `AMENDMENT` or `Gob Keng Swee` to `Goh Keng Swee`, or one printed office title to its ministry. A rule makes a link only when it finds exactly one target. Certain rules ship first, and a looser rule ships only after samples drawn for that rule, by decade and report type, show no wrong links. Similarity scores, fuzzy search and AI matching may not make a link. A similarity score does give the same answer each time, but nobody can say why 0.85 passes a pair and 0.84 fails it, while a misprint on a list can be read and argued with. AI may check links, under the terms of ADR 0009, and it may flag a problem but never make a link or clear a flag. The hand-kept lists change only the key used for matching, so the site still shows what the record prints (ADR 0006). The cost is that a link a fuzzy matcher would have found stays missing until someone adds a rule or a list entry, and the lists need upkeep. Eight Act links made by the similarity score on 5 November 2026 stay until they move to the hand-kept list. Royce made this ruling "for now", and changing it needs a new ADR.
